# Master Corpus Specification

**Project:** Japanese Corpus Analysis Project  
**Version:** 3.0  
**Status:** Active

---

# 1. Purpose

This specification defines the methodology used throughout the Japanese Corpus Analysis Project.

It establishes:

- evidence standards
- analysis procedures
- corpus metrics
- vocabulary classification
- Lesson Coach methodology
- corpus update procedures

This document defines **how** analysis is performed.

It intentionally contains **no corpus data**.

Corpus data belongs in the Corpus Statistics and Master Lexical Database.

---

# 2. Primary Objective

The project evaluates Japanese learning resources according to what repeated exposure is likely to teach rather than their advertised level.

The project seeks evidence for:

- structural fluency
- vocabulary acquisition
- discourse acquisition
- communicative competence
- transition readiness
- native input compatibility

The goal is to produce an evidence-based roadmap for progressing from learner material to native Japanese.

---

# 3. Evidence Framework

Every conclusion must be classified using one of the following categories.

## Observation

Supported directly by corpus evidence.

Examples include:

- frequency
- repetition
- distribution
- recurring grammar
- recurring discourse markers

Observations should contain no interpretation.

---

## Inference

Supported by multiple observations.

Inference explains patterns found in observations.

Inference must always reference the observations that support it.

---

## Hypothesis

A plausible explanation requiring additional evidence.

Hypotheses should never be presented as established fact.

Future corpus updates are expected to strengthen, weaken or reject hypotheses.

---

# 4. Confidence Ratings

Every major conclusion receives a confidence rating.

## High

Supported repeatedly across the corpus.

Conflicting evidence is minimal.

---

## Moderate

Supported by current evidence but may change as the corpus expands.

---

## Low

Limited supporting evidence.

Requires additional corpus analysis.

---

Confidence should increase only when new corpus evidence justifies it.

---

# 5. Corpus Analysis Categories

Every corpus update should analyze the following areas.

## Grammar

Track:

- recurring structures
- structural repetition
- structural stability
- grammatical complexity
- acquisition potential

Examples include:

- ～と思う
- ～という
- ～ということ
- ～こと
- ～ので
- ～ように
- quotation
- recommendation
- comparison
- explanation

---

## Vocabulary

Classify vocabulary into:

### Core Vocabulary

High frequency.

Widely distributed.

Appears across many communicative situations.

---

### Frequently Recycled Vocabulary

Moderate frequency.

Appears repeatedly across the corpus.

Likely to be naturally acquired.

---

### Topic Vocabulary

Limited to specific discussions.

Low transfer value.

---

### One-Off Vocabulary

Appears once or rarely.

Little evidence for prioritization.

---

## Discourse

Track:

- explanation
- comparison
- recommendation
- storytelling
- persuasion
- clarification
- listener engagement
- rhetorical questioning
- topic transitions

---

## Communicative Functions

Measure how frequently the corpus practices:

- giving opinions
- defining
- explaining
- comparing
- recommending
- persuading
- storytelling
- asking questions
- giving reasons
- categorizing

---

# 6. Corpus Metrics

The following metrics should be maintained.

## Structural Density Index (SDI)

Measures reusable grammar density.

Higher SDI indicates stronger structural reinforcement.

---

## Vocabulary Recycling Index (VRI)

Measures distributed lexical repetition.

Distributed repetition is weighted more heavily than clustered repetition.

---

## Topic Turnover Rate (TTR)

Measures how rapidly topical vocabulary disappears.

Higher turnover indicates lower acquisition priority.

---

## Structural Stability

Measures consistency of grammatical framework across episodes.

---

## Communicative Function Coverage (CFC)

Measures how broadly communicative functions are represented throughout the corpus.

---

## Transition Readiness Score (TRS)

Estimates readiness for the next learning resource.

Factors include:

- grammar overlap
- discourse overlap
- vocabulary overlap
- communicative overlap

---

## Native Input Compatibility (NIC)

Estimates similarity to native Japanese.

Considers:

- sentence structure
- discourse
- ellipsis
- repetition
- conversational flow

---

## Corpus Importance Score (CIS)

Ranks vocabulary according to overall acquisition value.

CIS combines:

- Frequency (25%)
- Distribution (35%)
- Grammar Association (15%)
- Communicative Breadth (15%)
- Topic Independence (10%)

CIS is preferred over raw frequency when determining study priority.

---

# 7. Lesson Coach Methodology

Lesson Coach reports should coach attention rather than summarize lessons.

Every report should identify:

- Primary learning objective
- High priority structures
- Emerging structures
- High-value vocabulary
- Topic vocabulary
- Communicative function
- Attention hierarchy
- Transfer to future Japanese
- Changes to the corpus model

Recommendations should be based on the **entire analyzed corpus**, not the lesson in isolation.

---

# 8. Vocabulary Management

Each tracked vocabulary item receives a learner status.

## Unknown

Not yet learned.

---

## Learning

Currently being acquired.

---

## Graduated (K)

Marked by the learner as mastered.

Graduated words:

- remain in corpus statistics
- remain in lexical analysis
- are excluded from Lesson Coach recommendations
- remain searchable within the Master Lexical Database

Graduation status affects coaching only.

It never alters corpus statistics.

---

# 9. Corpus Update Procedure

Each new corpus expansion should follow the same workflow.

1. Merge new episodes with the existing corpus.
2. Recalculate all frequency statistics.
3. Update the Master Lexical Database.
4. Recalculate corpus metrics.
5. Evaluate existing hypotheses.
6. Update confidence ratings where warranted.
7. Record changes in the Corpus Changelog.
8. Regenerate Corpus Statistics.
9. Generate Lesson Coach reports using the updated corpus.

Analysis should extend the previous corpus rather than restart it.

---

# 10. Corpus Comparison

Future corpora should be evaluated using the same methodology.

Comparison categories include:

- grammar overlap
- discourse overlap
- vocabulary overlap
- communicative functions
- lexical recycling
- structural repetition
- transition difficulty
- acquisition yield

Examples include comparisons between:

- Teppei Beginner
- Manga
- Anime
- Native podcasts
- Slice-of-life dialogue

---

# 11. Design Principles

The project follows these principles.

- Evidence over intuition.
- Repetition over novelty.
- Distribution over frequency.
- Structural fluency over vocabulary counts.
- Longitudinal analysis over isolated lessons.
- Revision when evidence changes.
- Transparency in confidence ratings.

---

# 12. Long-Term Vision

The completed project should become a searchable acquisition database capable of identifying:

- the most valuable vocabulary
- the most transferable grammar
- the highest-yield corpora
- optimal transition points between resources
- long-term acquisition patterns
- learner-specific study priorities

The project should continue to evolve as additional corpora are incorporated.
